# Ranking metrics beyond recall

Recall alone rewards a retriever that returns everything. Mean reciprocal rank punishes a system that
finds the right answer at position nine as much as one that never finds it, because the contribution
is one over the rank.

## Graded relevance

Useful answers are not binary. A three level scale - must hit, should hit, related - lets the report
distinguish a missing core answer from a missing side note. Normalised discounted cumulative gain is
the metric that actually consumes those grades; recall@k only looks at the binary cut at grade two.

## Precision and negative samples

When a dataset lists explicitly irrelevant documents, precision@k becomes meaningful again and guards
against a retriever that pads results with noise. Without negative samples precision@k is close to
meaningless on a small corpus, so this project reports it only when negatives exist.

## Reporting discipline

Broken down by query type, a metric tells you where the retriever fails: purely lexical questions,
paraphrases without shared vocabulary, type filtered questions, and scoped questions each fail for
different reasons. An average over all of them hides all of it.
